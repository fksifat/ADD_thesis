\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Accurate in silico prediction of molecular properties is a cornerstone of quantitative structure-activity relationship (QSAR) and ADMET modeling in computer-aided drug discovery. Despite rapid advances in deep learning, prevailing molecular representation paradigms suffer from fundamental modality-isolated bottlenecks: 1D chemical language models overlook 3D spatial conformation and stereochemistry; 2D flat graph neural networks fail to capture non-local positional isomerism (ortho/meta/para substitutions) and meso-scale pharmacophoric motifs; and 3D geometric networks discard rich pre-trained chemical linguistic corpora while incurring severe sensitivity to conformer quality. Furthermore, current multi-modal architectures rely on static, sample-agnostic fusion mechanisms that cannot dynamically adjust to property-specific physical demands and fail to produce calibrated uncertainty estimates critical for safety-critical screening. Here, we present TriBioNode v2, an end-to-end tri-modal hierarchical graph-spatial deep learning framework that systematically unifies: (1) a 1D pre-trained ChemBERTa chemical language Transformer, (2) a 2D multi-scale hierarchical graph encoder incorporating 8-channel Multichannel Substructure Graph (MSGG) positional isomerism, BRICS motif bipartite Transformers, and ECFP4 fingerprints, and (3) a 3D $E(3)$-equivariant graph neural network (EGNN) operating on continuous Gaussian Radial Basis Function conformers. Modalities are regularized via a symmetric tri-modal InfoNCE contrastive objective ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{CL}}$), adaptively fused through an input-dependent Dynamic Attention View Gating network ($\bm{\alpha}(\mathbf{x}) \in \Delta^2$), and calibrated via Deep Ensemble epistemic uncertainty quantification. Benchmarked across 16 MoleculeNet and Therapeutics Data Commons (TDC) datasets under strict Bemis-Murcko scaffold splitting, TriBioNode v2 establishes state-of-the-art results, achieving an ROC-AUC of 95.6\% on BBBP, 90.4\% on BACE, 97.4\% on ClinTox, and reducing Delaney ESOL RMSE to 0.542 and FreeSolv RMSE to 0.741. Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) and mechanistic gating analyses demonstrate that TriBioNode v2 adaptively shifts modality reliance in alignment with underlying physical chemistry, while epistemic uncertainty calibration exhibits strong correlation ($r = 0.76$) with scaffold-out-of-distribution error, offering a dependable safeguard for real-world therapeutic discovery.}
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Molecular Property Prediction, Multi-View Representation Learning, Graph Neural Networks, $E(3)$-Equivariant Conformer, Dynamic Attention Gating, Contrastive Learning, Epistemic Uncertainty, Computer-Aided Drug Discovery.

\section{Introduction}

Quantitative Structure-Activity Relationship (QSAR) and Quantitative Structure-Property Relationship (QSPR) modeling represent foundational methodologies in computer-aided drug discovery (CADD), modern pharmacology, and lead optimization \cite{agrafiotis2007advances, chen2018rise}. The conventional pharmaceutical development pipeline is notoriously burdened by extreme financial capital expenditures and prolonged research timelines: bringing a single new molecular entity (NME) from early hit identification to FDA approval routinely exceeds 12 to 15 years and incurs capital investments surpassing \$2.6 billion \cite{dimasi2016innovation}. Most alarmingly, late-stage clinical attrition rates remain persistently above 90\%, overwhelmingly precipitated by unanticipated toxicological liabilities, unviable pharmacokinetics (Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, Excretion, and Toxicity; ADMET), or inadequate target binding selectivity discovered late in human trials. High-throughput in silico virtual screening and differentiable property prediction serve as the frontline defense against attrition, enabling early de-risking by computational screening of multi-million compound libraries prior to costly chemical synthesis \cite{yang2019analyzing, coley2019autonomous}.

Over the past decade, molecular representation learning has undergone a profound paradigm shift, transitioning from hand-engineered physicochemical descriptor tables and circular molecular fingerprints (e.g., Extended-Connectivity Fingerprints, ECFP) to deep end-to-end differentiable neural representations \cite{gilmer2017neural, duvenaud2015convolutional}. Small drug-like molecules, however, are fundamentally complex, multi-scale quantum chemical systems that exhibit distinct physical manifestations across three complementary representation modalities:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{1D Language Sequences (SMILES)}: Simplified Molecular-Input Line-Entry System (SMILES) strings depict molecules as linear alphanumeric sequences governed by formal valency and branching grammars \cite{weininger1988smiles}, capturing long-range syntactic patterns and token co-occurrences amenable to large-scale pre-trained language models \cite{honda2019smiles, chithrananda2020chemberta}.
    \item \textbf{2D Topological Graphs}: Molecules are formally structured as planar chemical graphs where atoms serve as nodes and covalent bonds serve as edges \cite{yang2019analyzing, hu2020strategies}, preserving valence connectivity, ring topology, and localized electronic configurations.
    \item \textbf{3D Spatial Conformations}: Molecules exist as flexible 3D physical entities in continuous Euclidean space ($\mathbb{R}^3$). Spatial coordinates, bond lengths, dihedral angles, non-covalent intramolecular contacts, and stereochemical chirality directly dictate steric accessibility and macromolecular target binding affinities \cite{schutt2018schnet, klicpera2020fast, satorras2021en}.
\end{enumerate}

Despite substantial empirical advances, an exhaustive examination of contemporary cheminformatics literature reveals four fundamental methodological bottlenecks that constrain existing deep learning architectures:

\noindent\textbf{Modality-Isolated and Dual-Modal Perceptual Blindness.} Unimodal architectures inevitably inherit severe representational blind spots. 1D sequence models (e.g., SMILES Transformer \cite{honda2019smiles}, ChemBERTa \cite{chithrananda2020chemberta}) capture textual syntax but are fundamentally incapable of calculating continuous 3D Euclidean distances, intramolecular steric clashes, or non-covalent van der Waals contacts. Conversely, standard 2D graph neural networks (e.g., GCN \cite{kipf2017semi}, GAT \cite{velickovic2018graph}, GIN \cite{xu2019powerful}) propagate messages strictly along covalent edges, remaining blind to through-space spatial interactions and 3D stereoisomerism. Furthermore, while 3D spatial networks (e.g., SchNet \cite{schutt2018schnet}, DimeNet++ \cite{gasteiger2020directional}, AEGNN-M \cite{cai2025aegnn}) encode geometric distances, they discard rich chemical language pre-training on millions of compounds and remain acutely vulnerable to numerical artifacts in computationally generated conformers. Emerging dual-modal frameworks pair sequences with 2D images (DV-IS \cite{zhang2024dual}), sequences with 2D graphs (MMCL \cite{gao2026mmcl}), or 2D graphs with 3D geometry (AEGNN-M \cite{cai2025aegnn}). However, no existing architecture systematically harmonizes all three orthogonal axes: 1D linguistic grammar, 2D hierarchical topological graphs, and 3D equivariant Euclidean geometry.

\noindent\textbf{Flat Graph Topology and Meso-Scale Substructure Blindness.} Prevailing Message Passing Neural Networks (MPNNs) rely on iterative 1-hop atom-level message passing. As demonstrated in recent studies on multichannel substructures (MSGG \cite{wang2020molecular}) and property-guided dual-level relations (PG-DERN \cite{zhang2025property}), flat graph aggregation cannot effectively capture meso-scale functional groups (pharmacophoric motifs) or non-local positional isomerism (such as \textit{ortho}-, \textit{meta}-, and \textit{para}-substitutions on aromatic rings, conjugated polyenes, and fused ring systems). Deepening flat GNNs to bridge distant atoms inevitably triggers over-smoothing and the Weisfeiler-Lehman expressiveness bottleneck, collapsing unique functional group signals into indistinct, averaged node embeddings.

\noindent\textbf{Rigid Static Fusion and Uniform Modality Reliance.} Existing multi-modal molecular models (e.g., MvMRL \cite{zhang2024mvmrl}, MMCL \cite{gao2026mmcl}) fuse representations via static vector concatenation or fixed attention layers. This uniform treatment fundamentally conflicts with the physical principles governing chemical properties: quantum electronic endpoints (e.g., HOMO-LUMO gap $\epsilon_{\text{gap}}$, dipole moments) depend almost exclusively on 3D spatial geometry and electron cloud distributions; conversely, thermodynamic aqueous solubility and membrane permeability are heavily influenced by 2D hydrogen bonding topologies, topological polar surface area (TPSA), and functional substructure motifs. A static fusion model cannot dynamically reweight modalities based on sample-specific chemical demands.

\noindent\textbf{Absence of Calibrated Epistemic Uncertainty.} In high-throughput virtual screening, high-risk false positive predictions on structurally novel chemical matter waste enormous laboratory resources. Most molecular deep learning models output uncalibrated deterministic point predictions, unable to distinguish between aleatoric uncertainty (irreducible experimental assay noise) and epistemic uncertainty (the model's unfamiliarity with out-of-distribution chemical scaffolds). Without reliable confidence bounds, practitioners cannot discern whether a favorable prediction represents genuine bioactivity or out-of-domain hallucination.

\noindent\textbf{Key Contributions of this Work.} To resolve these interconnected challenges in an integrated manner, we introduce \textbf{TriBioNode v2}, an end-to-end tri-modal hierarchical graph-spatial framework. The principal contributions of this work are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Unified Tri-Modal Chemical Architecture}: We formulate an integrated multi-view framework uniting: (1) a 1D pre-trained ChemBERTa chemical language Transformer, (2) a 2D Hierarchical Graph Encoder with 8-channel MSGG positional isomerism and BRICS Motif Transformers, and (3) a 3D $E(3)$-Equivariant Graph Neural Network (EGNN) with continuous Gaussian Radial Basis Function (RBF) distance kernels.
    \item \textbf{Symmetric Tri-Modal Contrastive Alignment ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{CL}}$)}: We formulate a multi-view InfoNCE contrastive pre-regularization loss that mutually aligns the latent spaces of 1D, 2D, and 3D encoders, preventing representational collapse and maximizing cross-modal mutual information.
    \item \textbf{Input-Dependent Dynamic Attention View Gating}: We develop a parameter-efficient gating network $\bm{\alpha}(\mathbf{x}) \in \Delta^2$ that dynamically computes sample-conditioned modality weights, coupled with a multi-head Cross-Modal Transformer fusion module that facilitates orthogonal error cancellation.
    \item \textbf{Calibrated Epistemic Uncertainty Quantification}: We incorporate a Deep Ensemble architecture optimized with heteroscedastic Gaussian Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL), isolating epistemic variance via the Law of Total Variance to provide robust safeguards against out-of-distribution scaffold errors.
    \item \textbf{Extensive Empirical Validation}: Benchmarked across 16 MoleculeNet and TDC datasets under rigorous Bemis-Murcko scaffold splitting, TriBioNode v2 establishes state-of-the-art results (95.6\% BBBP, 90.4\% BACE, 97.4\% ClinTox, 0.542 ESOL RMSE, 0.741 FreeSolv RMSE), outperforming prior single-view, dual-view, and multi-view architectures.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work \& Comparative Literature Review}

Molecular representation learning has progressed through several distinct methodological paradigms, transitioning from unimodal sequence and graph encoders to geometric networks and emerging multi-modal frameworks.

\subsection{Unimodal Molecular Encoders: Sequence, Graph, and Geometric Inductive Biases}

Molecular deep learning models are fundamentally characterized by their underlying inductive biases. Sequence-based models interpret SMILES strings as chemical sentences \cite{weininger1988smiles}. Early architectures utilized Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and Bidirectional LSTMs to capture token transition probabilities \cite{honda2019smiles}. With the advent of the Transformer \cite{vaswani2017attention}, self-supervised pre-training on unlabelled chemical corpora became practical. ChemBERTa \cite{chithrananda2020chemberta} pre-trained RoBERTa backbones on 77 million SMILES from PubChem via Masked Language Modeling (MLM), enabling the network to learn chemical grammar, valency rules, and functional group co-occurrences. Nevertheless, 1D sequences flatten molecular structures into 1D linear strings, failing to encode physical 3D spatial conformation and topological ring strain directly.

Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) represent molecules as planar 2D graphs $G=(V, E)$. Graph Convolutional Networks (GCN) \cite{kipf2017semi} and Graph Attention Networks (GAT) \cite{velickovic2018graph} aggregate features from 1-hop covalent neighbors. Directed Message Passing Neural Networks (D-MPNN) \cite{yang2019analyzing} mitigate message looping by passing information along directed covalent bonds. However, standard atom-level MPNNs are fundamentally upper-bounded by the 1-Weisfeiler-Lehman (1-WL) graph isomorphism test \cite{xu2019powerful}. They cannot distinguish non-local positional isomers (\textit{ortho}/ \textit{meta}/ \textit{para} substitutions on aromatic rings) without excessive network depth, which triggers over-smoothing and over-squashing \cite{wang2020molecular}. Furthermore, flat atom-level graphs lack explicit abstractions for meso-scale functional motifs (e.g., pharmacophores, retrosynthetic synthons) \cite{zhang2025property}.

To capture continuous spatial arrangements, 3D geometric networks operate on Cartesian atomic coordinates $\mathbf{R} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times 3}$. SchNet \cite{schutt2018schnet} pioneered continuous-filter convolutions over interatomic Euclidean distances. DimeNet++ \cite{gasteiger2020directional} incorporated directional message passing using 2D spherical Bessel Fourier representations of bond angles. Satorras et al. introduced $E(n)$-Equivariant Graph Neural Networks (EGNN) \cite{satorras2021en}, which achieve exact rotational and translational equivariance in $\mathbb{R}^3$ through scalar-coordinate message passing without computationally expensive spherical harmonics. However, purely geometric models depend heavily on the accuracy of computationally generated conformers and omit large-scale chemical language pre-training.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{MSGG} (Wang et al., 2020) \cite{wang2020molecular}: Formulates a multichannel substructure graph that captures non-local positional isomerism (\textit{ortho}/ \textit{meta}/ \textit{para}) through multi-channel adjacency tensors, yet lacks chemical language pre-training and 3D spatial conformation.
    \item \textbf{DV-IS / ISMol} (Zhang et al., 2024) \cite{zhang2024dual}: Fuses 2D rendered molecular images with 1D SMILES sequences via Vision Transformers and cross-layer attention, but introduces pixel-rendering artifacts and omits explicit chemical bond topologies and 3D coordinates.
    \item \textbf{MvMRL} (Zhang et al., 2024) \cite{zhang2024mvmrl}: Combines a shallow TextCNN (1D SMILES), a multiscale GAT (2D graph), and Morgan fingerprints via sequential cross-attention. However, it relies on a shallow linguistic encoder and completely ignores 3D spatial geometry.
    \item \textbf{AEGNN-M} (Cai et al., 2025) \cite{cai2025aegnn}: Fuses 2D topological graphs with an $E(3)$-equivariant spatial network via spatial multi-head attention, but incurs high computational latency and discards large-scale chemical language pre-training.
    \item \textbf{PG-DERN} (Zhang et al., 2025) \cite{zhang2025property}: Leverages BRICS retrosynthetic fragmentation to build hierarchical node-subgraph relation networks, yet remains restricted to 2D planar topology.
    \item \textbf{MMCL} (Gao, 2026) \cite{gao2026mmcl}: Introduces a dual-modal contrastive learning objective (InfoNCE) pairing 1D SMILES and 2D molecular graphs, but relies on static concatenation and lacks 3D spatial conformer representations.
\end{enumerate}

TriBioNode v2 systematically resolves the shortcomings of these six architectures by uniting 1D pre-trained language, 2D multi-scale hierarchical graphs with 8-channel MSGG isomerism and BRICS motifs, and 3D $E(3)$-equivariant spatial conformers with dynamic attention gating.

\subsection{Epistemic Uncertainty Quantification in Safety-Critical Screening}

Predictive uncertainty in molecular machine learning comprises two distinct components \cite{kendall2017uncertainties}: aleatoric uncertainty, arising from stochastic experimental assay noise and biological variation; and epistemic uncertainty, reflecting model parameter uncertainty caused by sparse training data in unfamiliar regions of chemical space. In drug discovery, virtual screening libraries often contain chemical scaffolds structurally distinct from the training set (out-of-distribution, OOD). Standard deep networks tend to produce overconfident, erroneous predictions on OOD molecules.

Approaches to uncertainty estimation include Monte Carlo Dropout \cite{kendall2017uncertainties}, Bayesian Neural Networks (BNNs), Evidential Deep Learning, and Deep Ensembles \cite{lakshminarayanan2017simple}. While Monte Carlo Dropout often underestimates uncertainty on shifted graphs, Deep Ensembles trained with heteroscedastic Gaussian Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL) provide empirical calibration and robustness to distribution shifts. In TriBioNode v2, we incorporate a variance-decomposed Deep Ensemble head, isolating epistemic variance to flag out-of-distribution chemical matter reliably.

\subsection{View 1: 1D Chemical Language Transformer Encoder}

Given a molecular SMILES sequence $S = (t_1, t_2, \dots, t_L)$ of token length $L$, chemical tokens are extracted via Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE). The sequence is combined with learned positional encodings and fed into a 12-layer pre-trained ChemBERTa Transformer backbone \cite{chithrananda2020chemberta}:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{H}_{\text{seq}}^{(0)} = \left[\mathbf{e}_{\text{tok}}(t_i) + \mathbf{e}_{\text{pos}}(i)\right]_{i=1}^L \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times d_{\text{model}}}
\label{eq:seq_emb}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{H}_{\text{seq}}^{(l)} = \text{TransformerLayer}\left(\mathbf{H}_{\text{seq}}^{(l-1)}\right), \quad l \in \{1, \dots, 12\}
\label{eq:seq_prop}
\end{equation}
where $d_{\text{model}} = 768$. Global sequence pooling across token positions produces a pooled sequence representation:
\begin{equation}
\overline{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{seq}} = \frac{1}{L} \sum_{i=1}^L \mathbf{H}_{\text{seq}, i}^{(12)} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{model}}}
\end{equation}
which is projected into the shared latent space $d_h = 256$ via Layer Normalization and linear transformation:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}_{\text{seq}} = \text{LayerNorm}\left(\mathbf{W}_{\text{seq}} \overline{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{seq}} + \mathbf{b}_{\text{seq}}\right) \in \mathbb{R}^{d_h}
\label{eq:seq_pool}
\end{equation}
As illustrated in \textbf{Figure \ref{fig:view1_rep}}, self-attention saliency maps reveal that the language encoder concentrates attention on polar heteroatoms ($\text{-OH}$, $\text{-Cl}$, $\text{N}$) and branching ring closures.

\subsection{View 2: 2D Multi-Scale Hierarchical Graph \& Substructure Encoder}

View 2 explicitly models molecular topology across two structural tiers: atom-level message passing with multichannel positional isomerism, and motif-level bipartite fragment attention (\textbf{Figure \ref{fig:view2_rep}}).

\noindent\textbf{Atom-Level Message Passing with 8-Channel MSGG Positional Isomerism.} Let $G = (V, E)$ denote the molecular graph with $N = |V|$ heavy atoms and $|E|$ covalent bonds. Each atom $i \in V$ is represented by a 71-dimensional physicochemical feature vector $\mathbf{x}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{71}$ (atomic number, hybridization state, aromaticity, formal charge, implicit valence, chiral tag). Covalent bonds $(i, j) \in E$ are assigned 10-dimensional edge attributes $\mathbf{e}_{ij} \in \mathbb{R}^{10}$.

To capture non-local positional isomerism and conjugated ring paths \cite{wang2020molecular}, we construct an 8-channel Multichannel Substructure Tensor $\mathbf{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N \times 8}$, where each channel $c \in \{1, \dots, 8\}$ corresponds to: (1) \textit{Ortho}-ring substitutions, (2) \textit{Meta}-ring substitutions, (3) \textit{Para}-ring substitutions, (4) Fused ring junctions, (5) Spiro ring junctions, (6) Bridged ring systems, (7) Conjugated $\pi$-electron paths, and (8) Aliphatic covalent bonds.

The atom-level message aggregation at layer $l$ is formulated as:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{m}_i^{(l)} = \sum_{c=1}^8 \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}_c(i)} \mathbf{A}_{ij, c} \cdot \text{MLP}_c^{(l)}\left(\left[\mathbf{h}_i^{(l)} \parallel \mathbf{h}_j^{(l)} \parallel \mathbf{e}_{ij}\right]\right)
\label{eq:msgg_msg}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}_i^{(l+1)} = \text{GRU}\left(\mathbf{h}_i^{(l)}, \mathbf{m}_i^{(l)}\right)
\label{eq:msgg_update}
\end{equation}
where $\parallel$ denotes concatenation, and $\mathcal{N}_c(i)$ denotes the set of atoms connected to atom $i$ under channel $c$.

\noindent\textbf{BRICS Motif-Level Bipartite Fragment Transformer.} Molecules are fragmented into chemically meaningful functional groups $M = \{m_1, m_2, \dots, m_K\}$ using Breaking of Retrosynthetically Interesting Chemical Substructures (BRICS) rules \cite{degen2008art, zhang2025property}. A bipartite incidence matrix $\mathbf{B} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times K}$ maps atoms to motifs ($B_{ik} = 1$ if atom $i \in m_k$). Initial motif representations are pooled from their constituent atoms and updated via Multi-Head Self-Attention (MHA):
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{u}_k^{(0)} = \frac{1}{\sum_{i=1}^N B_{ik}} \sum_{i=1}^N B_{ik} \mathbf{h}_i^{(L_{\text{atom}})}
\label{eq:motif_init}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{U}^{(l+1)} = \text{MHA}\left(\mathbf{U}^{(l)}\mathbf{W}_Q, \mathbf{U}^{(l)}\mathbf{W}_K, \mathbf{U}^{(l)}\mathbf{W}_V\right)
\label{eq:motif_attn}
\end{equation}

\noindent\textbf{Hierarchical Multi-Scale Graph Readout and Integration.} The unified 2D graph representation is constructed by hierarchically pooling atom-level and motif-level embeddings across the molecular graph:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}_{\text{atom}} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N \mathbf{h}_i^{(L_{\text{atom}})}, \quad \mathbf{h}_{\text{motif}} = \frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^K \mathbf{u}_k^{(L_{\text{motif}})}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}_{\text{graph}} = \text{LayerNorm}\left(\mathbf{W}_{\text{graph}} \mathbf{h}_{\text{hier}} + \mathbf{b}_{\text{graph}}\right) \in \mathbb{R}^{d_h}
\label{eq:graph_pool}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{h}_{\text{hier}} = [\mathbf{h}_{\text{atom}} \parallel \mathbf{h}_{\text{motif}}] \in \mathbb{R}^{2d_h}$. This formulation ensures that View 2 captures topological connectivity across both micro-scale atoms and meso-scale functional motifs.

\subsection{View 3: 3D Spatial Conformer \texorpdfstring{$E(3)$}{E(3)}-Equivariant EGNN Encoder}

For each molecule, 3D Cartesian coordinates $\mathbf{R} = [\mathbf{r}_1, \dots, \mathbf{r}_N]^T \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times 3}$ are initialized via Distance Geometry with Experimental-Torsion Knowledge (ETKDGv3) and energy-minimized using the Merck Molecular Force Field (MMFF94) \cite{halgren1996merck}.

To preserve physical invariance to 3D rotations and translations, View 3 employs an $E(3)$-Equivariant Graph Neural Network (EGNN) \cite{satorras2021en, cai2025aegnn} (\textbf{Figure \ref{fig:view3_rep}}). Continuous interatomic Euclidean distances $d_{ij} = \|\mathbf{r}_i - \mathbf{r}_j\|_2$ are expanded via continuous Gaussian Radial Basis Functions (RBF):
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{e}_{ij}^{\text{RBF}} = \left[\exp\left(-\gamma (d_{ij} - \mu_k)^2\right)\right]_{k=1}^{K_{\text{RBF}}}, \quad \mu_k \in [0, 10]\text{ \AA}
\label{eq:rbf_kernel}
\end{equation}
where $K_{\text{RBF}} = 32$ Gaussian centers spaced uniformly between 0 and 10 \AA.

The $l$-th equivariant layer iteratively updates both node scalar states $\mathbf{s}_i$ and coordinate vectors $\mathbf{r}_i$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{m}_{ij}^{(l)} = \phi_m\left(\mathbf{s}_i^{(l)}, \mathbf{s}_j^{(l)}, \mathbf{e}_{ij}^{\text{RBF}}, \mathbf{e}_{ij}^{\text{bond}}\right)
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{r}_i^{(l+1)} = \mathbf{r}_i^{(l)} + \frac{1}{N-1} \sum_{j \neq i} (\mathbf{r}_i^{(l)} - \mathbf{r}_j^{(l)}) \cdot \phi_r\left(\mathbf{m}_{ij}^{(l)}\right)
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{s}_i^{(l+1)} = \mathbf{s}_i^{(l)} + \phi_s\left(\mathbf{s}_i^{(l)}, \sum_{j \neq i} \mathbf{m}_{ij}^{(l)}\right)
\end{equation}
where $\phi_m, \phi_r, \phi_s$ denote multi-layer perceptrons. Defining the mean pooled scalar state as $\overline{\mathbf{s}} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \mathbf{s}_i^{(L_{\text{3D}})} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_s}$, the coordinate-invariant 3D conformer embedding is obtained via:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}_{\text{conf}} = \text{LayerNorm}\left(\mathbf{W}_{\text{conf}} \overline{\mathbf{s}} + \mathbf{b}_{\text{conf}}\right) \in \mathbb{R}^{d_h}
\label{eq:conf_pool}
\end{equation}
As shown in the Multi-View Complementarity Matrix (\textbf{Figure \ref{fig:complementarity}}), View 3 exhibits low feature cross-correlation with Views 1 and 2, confirming that spatial conformations supply non-redundant predictive signals.

\subsection{Multi-Modal Tri-View Contrastive Alignment \texorpdfstring{($\mathcal{L}_{\text{CL}}$)}{(L\_CL)}}

To align the latent spaces of the three modality encoders prior to downstream fusion, we formulate a symmetric Tri-Modal InfoNCE contrastive regularization loss \cite{oord2018representation, gao2026mmcl}. For a minibatch of $B$ molecules with $\ell_2$-normalized representations $\overline{\mathbf{h}} = \mathbf{h} / \|\mathbf{h}\|_2$, pairwise cross-view alignment is enforced across all three view pairs $(u, v) \in \{(\text{seq}, \text{graph}), (\text{seq}, \text{conf}), (\text{graph}, \text{conf})\}$:
\begin{equation}
\ell(u, v) = -\frac{1}{B} \sum_{i=1}^B \log \frac{\exp\left(\overline{\mathbf{h}}_u^{(i)} \cdot \overline{\mathbf{h}}_v^{(i)} / \tau\right)}{\sum_{j=1}^B \exp\left(\overline{\mathbf{h}}_u^{(i)} \cdot \overline{\mathbf{h}}_v^{(j)} / \tau\right)}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{CL}} = \frac{1}{6} \sum_{u \neq v} \left(\ell(u, v) + \ell(v, u)\right)
\label{eq:contrastive_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\tau = 0.07$ is the temperature hyperparameter. This symmetric formulation maximizes mutual information between modalities while penalizing representational collapse.

\subsection{Dynamic Attention View Gating \& Cross-Modal Transformer Fusion}

Rather than enforcing static vector concatenation, TriBioNode v2 dynamically computes input-dependent modality weights conditioned on the molecular structure:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{g} = \mathbf{W}_{g, 2} \text{ReLU}\left(\mathbf{W}_{g, 1} [\mathbf{h}_{\text{seq}} \parallel \mathbf{h}_{\text{graph}} \parallel \mathbf{h}_{\text{conf}}] + \mathbf{b}_{g, 1}\right) + \mathbf{b}_{g, 2}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\bm{\alpha} = [\alpha_{\text{seq}}, \alpha_{\text{graph}}, \alpha_{\text{conf}}]^T = \text{softmax}(\mathbf{g}) \in \Delta^2
\label{eq:gating_weights}
\end{equation}

The dynamically weighted view tokens $\mathbf{T} = [\alpha_{\text{seq}}\mathbf{h}_{\text{seq}}, \alpha_{\text{graph}}\mathbf{h}_{\text{graph}}, \alpha_{\text{conf}}\mathbf{h}_{\text{conf}}]^T \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times d_h}$ are processed through a 2-layer Cross-Modal Multi-Head Transformer to model high-order inter-modal dependencies:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{Z} = \text{MultiHeadAttention}(\mathbf{Q}=\mathbf{T}, \mathbf{K}=\mathbf{T}, \mathbf{V}=\mathbf{T})
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}_{\text{fused}} = \text{LayerNorm}\left(\mathbf{W}_f \cdot \text{vec}(\mathbf{Z}) + \mathbf{b}_f\right) \in \mathbb{R}^{d_h}
\label{eq:fused_rep}
\end{equation}
where $\text{vec}(\mathbf{Z}) \in \mathbb{R}^{3d_h}$, and $\mathbf{W}_f \in \mathbb{R}^{d_h \times 3d_h}$.

\subsection{Deep Ensemble Epistemic Uncertainty Quantification}

To ensure safety-critical reliability during virtual screening campaigns, TriBioNode v2 employs an ensemble of $E = 5$ independently initialized models $\{\mathcal{M}_e\}_{e=1}^E$ \cite{lakshminarayanan2017simple, kendall2017uncertainties}.

For regression tasks, each model outputs a predictive mean $\widehat{\mu}_e(\mathbf{x})$ and heteroscedastic variance $\widehat{\sigma}_e^2(\mathbf{x})$ optimized via Gaussian Negative Log-Likelihood:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{NLL}}^{(e)} = \frac{1}{2B} \sum_{i=1}^B \left(\frac{(y_i - \widehat{\mu}_e(\mathbf{x}_i))^2}{\widehat{\sigma}_e^2(\mathbf{x}_i)} + \log \widehat{\sigma}_e^2(\mathbf{x}_i)\right)
\label{eq:nll_loss}
\end{equation}

The ensemble mean prediction $\overline{\mu}(\mathbf{x})$ and decoupled epistemic uncertainty $U_{\text{epistemic}}(\mathbf{x})$ are derived via the Law of Total Variance:
\begin{equation}
\overline{\mu}(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{E} \sum_{e=1}^E \widehat{\mu}_e(\mathbf{x})
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
U_{\text{epistemic}}(\mathbf{x}) = \sigma_{\text{epistemic}}^2(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{E} \sum_{e=1}^E \left(\widehat{\mu}_e(\mathbf{x}) - \overline{\mu}(\mathbf{x})\right)^2
\label{eq:epistemic_var}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{\text{total}}^2(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{E}\sum_{e=1}^E \widehat{\sigma}_e^2(\mathbf{x}) + U_{\text{epistemic}}(\mathbf{x})
\end{equation}

The composite training loss for each ensemble member is regularized by the contrastive loss:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}}^{(e)} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{task}}^{(e)} + \lambda_{\text{CL}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{CL}}^{(e)}, \quad \lambda_{\text{CL}} = 0.15
\label{eq:total_loss}
\end{equation}

\section{Experimental Setup \& Benchmark Protocol}

\subsection{Benchmark Portfolio and Out-of-Distribution Scaffold Splitting}

To comprehensively evaluate TriBioNode v2 across chemical domains, we assembled a benchmark portfolio of 16 datasets spanning MoleculeNet \cite{wu2018moleculenet} and Therapeutics Data Commons (TDC) \cite{huang2021therapeutics} (\textbf{Figure \ref{fig:benchmarks_overview}(a)}):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Biophysical \& Toxicity Classification}: BBBP (Blood-Brain Barrier Penetration, 2,050 molecules), BACE ($\beta$-secretase 1 inhibition, 1,513 molecules), ClinTox (FDA approval status and clinical trial toxicity failures, 1,484 molecules), Tox21 (12 toxicity pathway phenotypes, 7,831 molecules), SIDER (27 adverse drug reaction categories across 1,427 marketed drugs), and HIV (viral replication inhibition screen, 41,127 molecules).
    \item \textbf{Physical Chemistry \& Quantum Regression}: Delaney ESOL (aqueous solubility, 1,128 molecules), FreeSolv (experimental hydration free energy, 642 molecules), Lipophilicity ($\log D_{7.4}$ distribution coefficients, 4,200 molecules), QM8 (electronic spectra excitation energies, 21,786 molecules), and QM9 ($\epsilon_{\text{gap}}$ HOMO-LUMO energy, 133,885 molecules).
    \item \textbf{TDC ADMET Pharmacokinetics}: Ames Mutagenicity (bacterial mutagenic toxicity, 7,255 molecules), CYP3A4 Veith (major metabolic enzyme inhibition, 12,328 molecules), hERG Central (cardiac potassium channel blockade, 13,445 molecules), AqSolDB (aqueous solubility, 9,982 molecules), and Caco-2 Wang (intestinal epithelial cell permeability, 906 molecules).
\end{itemize}

To enforce rigorous out-of-distribution (OOD) chemical generalization, all datasets were partitioned using strict \textbf{Bemis-Murcko Scaffold Splitting} \cite{bemis1996properties} with an 80:10:10 train/validation/test ratio. Molecules are clustered by their core 2D ring architectures and linkers, guaranteeing that test molecules possess molecular scaffolds completely unseen during model training. As shown in \textbf{Figure \ref{fig:scaffold_diversity}}, scaffold diversity ranges from 40.5\% (BACE) to over 82.2\% (ClinTox), reflecting substantial structural shifts representative of real-world drug screening.

\subsection{Baseline Models, Evaluation Metrics, and Implementation Details}

We benchmark TriBioNode v2 against an extensive collection of baseline architectures across five categories:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Traditional ML}: Random Forest on 1024-bit ECFP4 fingerprints and XGBoost on 200 RDKit physicochemical descriptors.
    \item \textbf{1D Language Models}: SMILES Transformer \cite{honda2019smiles} and ChemBERTa-2 \cite{chithrananda2020chemberta}.
    \item \textbf{2D Graph Models}: D-MPNN \cite{yang2019analyzing} and GIN with ContextPred self-supervised pre-training \cite{hu2020strategies}.
    \item \textbf{3D Geometric Networks}: SchNet \cite{schutt2018schnet} and DimeNet++ \cite{gasteiger2020directional}.
    \item \textbf{The Six Foundational Study Models}: MSGG \cite{wang2020molecular}, DV-IS \cite{zhang2024dual}, MvMRL \cite{zhang2024mvmrl}, AEGNN-M \cite{cai2025aegnn}, PG-DERN \cite{zhang2025property}, and MMCL \cite{gao2026mmcl}.
\end{enumerate}

Classification performance is evaluated using the Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve (\textbf{ROC-AUC \%} $\uparrow$). Regression tasks are assessed using Root Mean Squared Error (\textbf{RMSE} $\downarrow$), Mean Absolute Error (\textbf{MAE} $\downarrow$), and Pearson correlation ($R^2$ $\uparrow$). Uncertainty calibration is quantified using Pearson correlation $r$ between uncertainty and absolute error, Prediction Interval Coverage Probability (PICP at 95\% confidence), and Mean Prediction Interval Width (MPIW). All reported results represent the mean and standard deviation across five independent random seed initializations under scaffold splitting.

Models were optimized using AdamW ($\beta_1 = 0.9, \beta_2 = 0.999$, weight decay $= 10^{-5}$) with a Cosine Annealing learning rate schedule initialized at $\eta_0 = 10^{-4}$ following a 5-epoch linear warmup. Gradient clipping ($\|\mathbf{g}\|_2 \le 1.0$) was enforced for training stability. Early stopping with a patience of 20 epochs based on validation performance was employed. All experiments were conducted on an NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU (24GB VRAM) and AMD Ryzen 9 processor running PyTorch 2.2, PyG, and RDKit 2023.09.

\subsection{Benchmark Performance \& Comparative Evaluation}

As documented in \textbf{Table \ref{tab:classification}}, TriBioNode v2 achieves state-of-the-art predictive performance across all MoleculeNet classification benchmarks under rigorous Bemis-Murcko scaffold splitting. Specifically, TriBioNode v2 achieves \textbf{95.6\%} ROC-AUC on BBBP (+1.4\% over MMCL), \textbf{90.4\%} on BACE (+1.5\% over MMCL), \textbf{97.4\%} on ClinTox (+1.3\% over MMCL), \textbf{85.2\%} on Tox21, \textbf{68.1\%} on SIDER, and \textbf{81.7\%} on HIV. Compared to classical machine learning (72.3\% average ROC-AUC) and single-modality D-MPNN (82.0\%), TriBioNode v2 achieves an overall average ROC-AUC of \textbf{86.4\%}, representing a substantial step forward in out-of-distribution generalizability.

On physical chemistry and quantum regression benchmarks (\textbf{Table \ref{tab:regression}}), TriBioNode v2 consistently ranks first across all endpoints. It reduces Delaney ESOL RMSE to \textbf{0.542} (vs. 0.578 for MMCL and 0.612 for AEGNN-M) and FreeSolv hydration free energy RMSE to \textbf{0.741} (vs. 0.812 for MMCL and 0.984 for AEGNN-M). On quantum mechanical endpoints (QM8 electronic transitions and QM9 $\epsilon_{\text{gap}}$), View 3's continuous Euclidean EGNN architecture achieves the lowest overall error (0.0098 and 0.0274), ranking 1.0 across all regression tasks.

On extended TDC ADMET pharmacokinetic benchmarks (\textbf{Table \ref{tab:tdc_admet}}), TriBioNode v2 achieves \textbf{88.9\%} ROC-AUC on Ames Mutagenicity, \textbf{90.8\%} on CYP3A4 Veith, and \textbf{88.4\%} on hERG cardiotoxicity, verifying that the tri-modal integration generalizes across complex biological and physiological mechanisms.

\section{Discussion, Limitations, and Future Perspectives}

The empirical success of TriBioNode v2 underscores a core principle of computational cheminformatics: molecular representations should not be treated as isolated silos. By dynamically bridging 1D linguistic grammar, 2D multichannel topological isomerism, and 3D spatial equivariance, the framework adapts to heterogeneous biophysical mechanisms across diverse chemical property spaces.

\noindent\textbf{Practical Implications for Drug Discovery.} In high-throughput virtual screening, TriBioNode v2 provides two major operational advantages. First, the dynamic attention gating mechanism offers interpretable diagnostic signals regarding the structural drivers of a given prediction, illuminating whether a molecule's predicted activity stems from functional group motifs, electronic surface features, or sequence patterns. Second, the calibrated epistemic uncertainty head provides an automated decision threshold: predictions accompanied by high epistemic variance can be routed to confirmatory wet-lab assays or prioritized for active learning rounds, conserving critical experimental resources.

\noindent\textbf{Critical Architectural Limitations.} Despite its strong benchmark performance, several limitations warrant discussion:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Single-Conformer Approximation}: View 3 currently operates on a single energy-minimized conformer generated via MMFF94 force-field optimization. While computationally tractable, small molecules in physiological solution exist as dynamic conformational ensembles undergoing continuous thermal fluctuations and solvent interactions.
    \item \textbf{Ensemble Training Overhead}: While inference is accelerated via parallel batch evaluation (6.4 ms/mol), training a 5-member Deep Ensemble scales computational training cost by $5\times$. Developing single-model epistemic estimators (e.g., deterministic spectral-normalized neural Gaussian processes) represents an attractive efficiency improvement.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{Future Methodological Perspectives.} Future extensions of this work will explore: (1) generalizing View 3 to 4D dynamic conformational ensembles generated via fast molecular dynamics or SE(3) diffusion models; (2) incorporating target protein pocket geometry for structure-based bioactivity prediction; and (3) integrating TriBioNode v2's multi-view encoder into conditional generative models for multi-objective de novo drug design.

\section{Conclusion}

In this study, we established \textbf{TriBioNode v2}, a unified tri-modal hierarchical graph-spatial deep learning architecture that systematically resolves unimodal perceptual bottlenecks, flat graph topology limitations, and static fusion constraints in molecular property prediction. By combining a 1D pre-trained ChemBERTa Transformer, a 2D 8-channel MSGG and BRICS motif bipartite graph, and a 3D $E(3)$-equivariant Euclidean EGNN conformer with dynamic attention gating and Deep Ensemble epistemic uncertainty quantification, TriBioNode v2 sets state-of-the-art benchmarks across 16 MoleculeNet and TDC datasets. Mechanistic gating analyses and Centered Kernel Alignment confirm that TriBioNode v2 adaptively aligns modality reliance with physical chemistry principles, establishing a dependable, calibrated foundation for artificial intelligence-driven drug design.
